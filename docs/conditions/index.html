Key Clinical Takeaways (Diagnostic Summary)

Clinical Management Principles Across Dermatological Phenotypes

  • •
    Barrier Integrity First: For active acne and erythematous rosacea, repairing the stratum corneum barrier reduces follicular hyperkeratinization and neurovascular flushing before initiating high-strength retinoids or keratolytics.
  • •
    Excipient Screening: High-risk comedogenic esters (e.g., Isopropyl Myristate) and volatile drying alcohols (Alcohol Denat >5%) must be eliminated in reactive phenotypes to avoid rebound seborrhea.
  • •
    Synergistic Photoprotection: Mineral Zinc Oxide combined with antioxidant Vitamin C and Niacinamide provides essential protection against matrix metalloproteinase-1 (MMP-1) collagen degradation and pigment stimulation.

1. Acne Vulgaris & Microcomedones

Clinical Code: ICD-11 FA40

A multifactorial inflammatory disease of the pilosebaceous unit characterized by abnormal follicular keratinization, sebum hyperproduction, Cutibacterium acnes proliferation, and perifollicular immune activation.

First-Line Clinical Actives

  • Niacinamide (4-5%): Suppresses cutaneous sebum excretion rate and blocks inflammatory IL-8 cytokines.
  • Tri-Enzyme Cleavage Complex: Keratolytic exfoliation eliminating cellular cohesion without chemical stripping.
  • Zinc Oxide (9.0%+): Non-comedogenic physical barrier with soothing zinc ions.

Contraindicated Excipients

  • Isopropyl Isostearate & Myristyl Myristate (Rabbit ear comedogenic score 4-5).
  • Heavy Occlusive Petrolatum without emulsifiers on active cystic lesions.
  • Artificial synthetic fragrances containing irritating cinnamal allergens.

2. Stratum Corneum Barrier Disruption & Elevated TEWL

Clinical Code: Biophysical Impairment

Depletion of the intercellular stratum corneum lipid matrix (Ceramides, Cholesterol, and Free Fatty Acids). Results in transepidermal water evaporation (TEWL > 25 g/m²/h), dehydration, burning sensations, and pathogen translocation.

First-Line Clinical Actives

  • Ceramide NP, AP, EOP: Essential sphingolipids restoring lipid bilayer stacking.
  • Prebiotic Thermal Springs & Postbiotics: Accelerates microbiome recovery and strengthens cornified envelope.
  • Phytosphingosine & Cholesterol: Lipophilic precursors for endogenous ceramide synthesis.

Contraindicated Excipients

  • High-concentration surfactant Sodium Lauryl Sulfate (SLS).
  • Denatured alcohol (Alcohol Denat) in concentrations > 5%.
  • Unbuffered low pH direct AHA/BHA chemical peels during acute barrier flare-ups.

3. Cutaneous Photoaging, Solar Elastosis & Collagen Loss

Clinical Code: ICD-11 EJ20

Chronic ultraviolet (UVA/UVB) and High-Energy Visible (HEV) radiation exposure inducing reactive oxygen species (ROS), AP-1 transcription activation, and matrix metalloproteinase-mediated Type I and III collagen degradation.

First-Line Clinical Actives

  • Broad-Spectrum Mineral Filters: Micronized Zinc Oxide (≥9.0%) blocking 290–400nm radiation.
  • 20% L-Ascorbic Acid: Neutralizes reactive hydroxyl radicals and acts as an essential cofactor for prolyl hydroxylase.
  • Marine Algae Peptides (Padina Pavonica): Stimulates glycosaminoglycan (GAG) dermal matrix synthesis.

Contraindicated Excipients

  • Phototoxic essential oils (Bergapten-rich Citrus oils).
  • Unprotected photosensitizing AHA exfoliation without daily broad-spectrum SPF 30+.
  • Degraded, oxidized Vitamin C formulations (brown quinone discoloration).

4. Facial Erythema, Rosacea & Vascular Reactivity

Clinical Code: ICD-11 ED90

Neurovascular dysregulation, epidermal cathelicidin LL-37 cleavage overactivity, and demodex folliculorum hypersensitivity leading to persistent centrofacial erythema, telangiectasias, and stinging sensations.

First-Line Clinical Actives

  • Transparent Zinc Oxide: Calms cutaneous microvasculature without chemical heat generation.
  • Niacinamide (Nicotinamide): Downregulates cutaneous vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF).
  • Colloidal Oatmeal & Prebiotics: Decreases histamine release and reduces stinging scores.

Contraindicated Excipients

  • Chemical UV filters (Avobenzone, Oxybenzone) that release thermal energy upon UV absorption.
  • Astringents containing Menthol, Camphor, Eucalyptus, or Witch Hazel.
  • Abrasive jagged physical scrubs (crushed walnut, apricot pits).
